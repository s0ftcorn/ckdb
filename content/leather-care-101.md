## WORK IN PROGRESS

Leather care, be it polish or anything else, helps to preserve your precious leather good by protecting it.  
Side note: It does *not* nourish it. There are a few conditioners with actual tannines in them, but whether they help or not is questionable and any damage done to your leather good is permanent. Enjoy the patina.
## 80% of the result in 20% the time
- Compressed air: Should you have access to it, be it in a can, use it. Especially for difficult to get into spots. Between tongue and vamp, welt and inside.
- Dry brush: Preferred are bristles that are not too aggressive, but not too soft. We dont want to polish (just yet). Even though brass bristles are often times recommend for suede leather, i wouldnt use them unless you know a little bit what you are doing.
	- Try to get into the corners with the brush, be a bit careful brushing the stitching. Cant really do much damage.
	- Try to get every bit of dirt. Sand and other stuff can be abrasive and we want to keep our precious leather fibers <3
- Damp cloth: No remotely abrasive stuff. Old t-shirt or towel, slightly damp, not dripping wet. 
	- Test it first on a area where you dont mind affecting the color of the leather.
	- Rub it with not much pressure, we dont want to make the leather damp. Just get the dirt out of it.
	- Even if your leather doesnt look damp, let it dry. Never try to speed up the drying of leather.
- At the end some kind of leather care product. Wax, milk, oil, grease. Many possibilities, always try it on a discreet spot. Almost every product will darken your leather. Sprays only for suede.
	- Less is more. Try to get minimal amount of product onto the leather and spread it. Its very easy putting more product on, but very hard getting it of.
	- Very soft polishing brush and buff it. Depending on product it can get quite glossy or silky.
	- If you want you can try around to combine different products. Example: Oil first, then wax.

## Getting into details
### Assess
Depending on dirt, leather or damage different approaches have proven to work well.
#### Type of leather
- Does it have a plastic-like feeling to it? Cracks rather than creases? Color didnt change much over time? Does it flake off? Maybe really shiny?
	- Then you are dealing with some kind of patent leather. ([[doc-martens]] would be the classic example)
- You rub it or scratch it lightly and it changes its colour? Looks like it has a little bit of fluff?
	- Depending on how much fluff or even fibers, it might be fleshout, split or nubuk leather.
- Rubbing it with your fingers, makes it more glossy or some light scrathes disappear? It seems to kind of self condition? Its a bit dull? Maybe even feels oily or waxy?
	- Waxed/oiled leather. Wax is more glossy, oil more dull. Or maybe even Shell Cordovan.
- Feels smooth? Scratches tear of tiny bits looking like paper? Creases but doesnt crack? Maybe feels kind of "natural"?
	- Full grain leather. The classic.
Keep in mind that apart from patent leather these types can be combined. Waxing the flesh side is a classic. Oiled full grain leather, too. Depending on the type of leather and the look you want to achieve, different approaches are needed.

#### The Damage and how to deal with it
- Water. Let the leather dry on its own. Do not heat it up. Do not put into direct sunlight. Do not stuff anything into it, okay maybe a small paper ball to keep it open. Do not use a hair dryer, even on the cold setting. You want it to dry on its own with the time it needs. Okay maybe not so slow that mold becomes a problem, but you get the point.
- Mud. Let it dry, brush it of. If you cant avoid it, use water and flush it away.
- Sand. Brush it of. Compressed air works wonders too.
- Oil. Act quickly, remove the majority of it with a cloth or towel. Then use sand to suck the oil out of the leather. There are special sands for sucking up oil, dont know the name of them currently.
- Sauces and other food related stuff: Wipe the majority off, then brush the remaining stuff away.
- Light scratches: buff it out for smooth leather, brush it away for fleshout or nubuk. 
- Deep scratches: 
	- Smooth leather and patent leather: Apply wax, buff it.
	- Rough leather: brass brushes will damage the lather, but also help making the scratch invisible.
- Cuts: You are fucked. Once leather is cut, its cut. Maybe a leatherworker can patch it up. Seems like you know one.

### Conditioners and cleaners
There are many, some are combinations, what works best is a bit of trial and error. *Always* try out the conditioner in a spot you dont mind looking like shit.  
Less is always more. Its easy to apply more but a huge pain in the ass to get it of.  
Always clean the leather before applying any kind of conditioner.  
- Apply lightly, use a cloth to wipe away the excess.
- Wait a few minutes or hours. No heat, no fans, no direct sunlight.
- Want it shiny? Buff it. Want it dull? Dont. Want it even more shiny? Re-do the application.
#### General Recommendations
Generally products made by [Saphir](https://saphir.com/)  and [Tapir]https://tapir.de/shop/lederpflege/) 
Dont want your leather to darken? [Bick 4](https://www.amazon.de/Bickmore-Bick-Leather-Conditioner-Unzen/dp/B001CS2Q4M)
#### Leather soap or saddle soap.
Should be used *only* when nothing else helps. It will strip away some of the tanning compounds in the leather.  
Get some on a brush or sponge, froth it up with some water and ... well, clean your leather.  
Afterwards wash away any excess with water. Let it dry on its own.
A more mild alternative is the Saphir Cleanser or Famaco Cleaning Foam
#### Cleaners in general
Should be used *only* when nothing else helps. It too will strip away some of the tanning compounds in the leather.
#### Oil
Popular choices: Mink Oil, Neatsfoot Oil. Historical note: linseed oil.  
Often some kind of oil is combined with other stuff.  
Apply it lightly, getting this stuff of is nearly impossible, applying more is easy.
Tips for applying lightly: Use a cloth, soak it in a spot, wait a bit for the capilarry action, and then apply the oil lightly.  
Some oils contain differect waxes, though a high shine polish is hard to achieve.  
#### Grease/Dubbin
Classic use case: mountain boots.  
Sometimes with color pigments, most of the time without. *Do not* use heat to "melt" the dubbin into the leather.  
Apply lightly, wait a few hours, buff it.  
Usually leads to more dull of a finish.  
#### Cream/Balm
Balms are usually without color pigmets, while creams are often colored. Depending on the ingredients (hard wax), a mirror polish can be achieved.  
Apply lightly, wait a few minutes or hours, buff it. If more shine is desired, re-do the application.  
Can almost always be buffed to a nice shine.
### Milk
Its called milk, because it looks like it. A thick but runny cream. Kind of a in-between of grease, cram and oil. Weird stuff, works suprisingly well.  
Recommended products: Chamberlains No. 1, Burgol Leather milk.
